Wireless security

Assess your own access point without disrupting nearby networks.

Guidebook homeLab setup
Radio traffic crosses property boundaries. Network names are not proof of ownership. Do not deauthenticate clients, attempt WPS PIN recovery, capture credentials, or inject frames without explicit authorization covering those actions.

Check hardware and normal connectivity

rfkill list
iw dev
nmcli device status
nmcli --fields IN-USE,SSID,CHAN,RATE,SIGNAL,SECURITY device wifi list

Monitor mode and packet injection require compatible hardware and drivers. A regular laptop adapter may support neither, and that is not a software bug.

Review your access point safely

  • Use WPA3-Personal when every required client supports it; otherwise use WPA2-AES.
  • Disable WPS unless there is a documented operational need.
  • Use a long, unique passphrase and change vendor-default administrator credentials.
  • Install supported firmware updates and remove obsolete client devices.
  • Separate guest and untrusted IoT devices from trusted systems.
  • Confirm remote administration is disabled unless intentionally secured.

Capture only your lab traffic

Wireshark can inspect traffic from your connected interface without placing it in monitor mode. Apply a display filter for your authorized lab host and stop capture as soon as the question is answered. Store captures like other sensitive evidence because they may contain identifiers or session data.

Document limitations

Record adapter model, driver, channel, access-point model, firmware version, security mode, and test distance. Wireless results are difficult to reproduce without environmental context.
